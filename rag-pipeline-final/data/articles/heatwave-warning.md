The city has escalated its heat advisory to a heat emergency after temperatures reached 42 degrees Celsius on Saturday, three degrees above the weekend's original forecast, with little overnight relief expected before Monday.

Officials are urging residents to stay hydrated, avoid strenuous activity entirely during daylight hours, and check on elderly neighbors and those without air conditioning. Two additional cooling centers have opened, bringing the citywide total to eight, now operating around the clock through the emergency period.

Emergency officials repeated warnings that parked cars can become dangerous within minutes and reminded residents never to leave children or pets inside a vehicle. Outdoor work has been suspended citywide during peak afternoon hours under the emergency declaration.

Cooler air is now expected to move in by Tuesday, a day later than originally forecast, bringing temperatures back to seasonal levels.
